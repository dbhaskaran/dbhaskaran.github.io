Pickleball Learning Program

A structured path from complete beginner to 4.0 rated player. Follow this step-by-step program with free video tutorials.

USAPA Rating Scale Reference

  • 1.0 Just learned the rules, minimal experience
  • 2.0 Can sustain short rallies, learning basic positioning
  • 2.5 Consistent on basic shots, understands court positioning
  • 3.0 Can place serves/returns, understands stacking & the kitchen
  • 3.5 Consistent dinks, developing 3rd shot drops, strategic play
  • 4.0 Controls pace, reliable drops & drives, smart shot selection

Program Overview

  1. Phase 1: Foundation (Weeks 1-3) — Rating: 1.0 → 2.0
  2. Phase 2: Building Consistency (Weeks 4-7) — Rating: 2.0 → 2.5
  3. Phase 3: Strategic Play (Weeks 8-12) — Rating: 2.5 → 3.0
  4. Phase 4: Advanced Fundamentals (Weeks 13-18) — Rating: 3.0 → 3.5
  5. Phase 5: Competitive Play (Weeks 19-26) — Rating: 3.5 → 4.0

Getting Started: Equipment

🏓

Paddle

Start with a mid-weight (7.5-8.2 oz) composite paddle. Budget: $30-80 for beginners.

⚪

Balls

Outdoor: Dura Fast 40 or Franklin X-40. Indoor: Onix Fuse or Jugs.

👟

Court Shoes

Tennis or volleyball shoes with lateral support. No running shoes!

👓

Accessories

Sweatband, water bottle, overgrip tape. Sunglasses for outdoor play.

Weeks 1-3

Week 1: Rules, Grip & Basic Rally

Understand court dimensions and zones (kitchen/NVZ)
Learn scoring system (side-out scoring for doubles)
Continental grip (hammer grip)
Ready position and athletic stance
Basic forehand push/block
Basic backhand push/block

Drills (30 min/session, 3x per week)

  • Wall rallies: 50 consecutive forehand, then 50 backhand
  • Self-feed and catch: toss ball up, let bounce, hit to target
  • Partner rally from mid-court: just keep the ball in play

Weeks 4-5: Dinking Fundamentals

Forehand dink (soft, controlled, over the net)
Backhand dink
Cross-court dinking patterns
Dink footwork (small adjustment steps)
Paddle face angle control
Patience: not attacking every ball

Drills

  • Cooperative dink rallies: 20+ consecutive cross-court
  • Figure-8 dinking: alternate forehand cross, backhand cross
  • Target dinking: place targets in NVZ corners, aim for them
  • Solo wall dink practice: soft touch 6 feet from wall

Weeks 6-7: Groundstrokes & Transition

Forehand groundstroke with topspin
Backhand groundstroke (one or two-handed)
Depth control on groundstrokes
The split step before every shot
Transitioning from baseline to kitchen line
Identifying when to stay back vs. move up

Drills

  • Groundstroke depth: hit past the service line consistently
  • Transition drill: return serve, hit 3rd shot, move to NVZ
  • 3-shot rally: serve, return, groundstroke — focus on deep placement
  • Split step timing: partner feeds random, you split-step then react

Strategic Play
Rating Goal: 2.5 → 3.0
Weeks 8-12

Weeks 8-9: The Third Shot (Drop & Drive)

Third shot drop concept and execution
Third shot drive (when opponents are back)
Decision making: drop vs. drive
Drop shot arc and trajectory
Follow your drop to the net
Resetting when the drop pops up

Drills

  • Drop shot from baseline: land in opponent's NVZ 5/10 times
  • Drop and transition: drop, then move forward, hit volley
  • Partner feeds deep, you choose drop or drive based on their position
  • Dead dink drill: hit drops that don't bounce above net height

Weeks 10-11: Court Positioning & Communication

Stacking in doubles (when and why)
Moving as a unit with your partner
Covering the middle (forehand takes)
Calling "mine," "yours," "out," "bounce"
Recognizing opponent patterns
Playing to opponent weaknesses

Drills

  • Shadow positioning: practice moving laterally with partner (no ball)
  • Middle ball drill: feeds down the middle, practice communication
  • 2-on-1 drill: two at kitchen vs. one at baseline practicing placement
  • Point play with focus: call every single ball verbally

Lob serve (high arc, deep)

Deep return + immediate advance to NVZ
Return placement variety

Drills

  • Return and rush: return deep, sprint to NVZ, split step

Advanced Fundamentals
Rating Goal: 3.0 → 3.5
Weeks 13-18

Weeks 13-14: Advanced Dinking & Resets

Dink with purpose: moving opponents laterally
Cross-court vs. line dinks (when to use each)
Attacking a high dink (speed-up)
Recognizing attackable balls
The reset shot (absorbing pace, resetting to NVZ)
Blocking hard drives from chest height

Drills

  • Dink-to-attack: dink rally until someone gets an attackable ball, speed up
  • Reset drill: partner drives hard, you reset softly into NVZ
  • 2-touch reset: absorb with paddle face open, redirect down
  • Random feed: partner alternates hard/soft, you choose appropriate response

Weeks 15-16: Lobs, Overheads & Erne

Offensive lob (over opponent's backhand)
Defensive lob (to buy recovery time)
Overhead smash technique
Recognizing lob opportunities
The Erne (jump/run around NVZ)
Defending against the Erne

Drills

  • Lob target practice: lob over partner at NVZ, land within 3 feet of baseline
  • Overhead drill: partner lobs, you put away with controlled overhead
  • Erne practice: dink rally, look for opportunity to Erne (practice footwork first without ball)
  • Defend the lob: partner lobs, you turn and run, hit reset or counter-lob

Rating Goal: 3.5 → 4.0

Weeks 19-21: Pace Control & Shot Selection Mastery

Controlling pace: speed up and slow down at will
Counter-attacking (blocking speed-ups back with purpose)
Two-handed backhand options
Inside-out forehand from middle
Approach shots (between drop and drive)
Disguising shots (same motion, different outcomes)

Drills

  • Shot selection game: coach/partner calls situation, you choose and execute
  • Disguise drill: practice same backswing for dink, drive, and lob

The 4.0 Difference: At 4.0, you don't just have all the shots — you know when to use them. Shot selection, consistency under pressure, and minimizing unforced errors are what separate 3.5 from 4.0. Play smart, not just hard.

Phase 5 — You're a 4.0 Player When:

  • Third shot drop is consistent (7/10) and you can drive with purpose
  • Dink rallies are weapons, not just neutral exchanges
  • You can counter speed-ups rather than just blocking
  • Unforced errors are rare (<3 per game)
  • You construct points with 2-3 shot sequences
  • You adapt strategy based on opponents within a game
  • You compete well in 3.5+ tournaments
  • You're mentally composed even in close games

Key Principles Throughout Your Journey

🎯

Consistency > Power

The player who makes fewer errors wins at every level. Keep the ball in play.

⏱

Patience Wins

Wait for the right ball to attack. Forcing shots leads to errors.

🥇

Get to the Net

80% of points are won at the kitchen line. Your goal is always to get there.

📚

Drill > Play

Playing is fun but drilling specific shots is how you actually improve.
